    Follow Flip

    A count-in, then Flip plays one bar. Then he waits for you. Play the bar back whenever you're ready; your first hit starts your turn, and it waits until you've played every hit. Get the right pads in the right order to pass; an even rhythm earns bonus points. Pass and you get a new bar. Miss and you hear the same bar again. As Flip plays, each hit shows up on screen so you can see what to copy. Pass three bars and they get harder; pass six and the tempo goes up 3 BPM. Three misses and you're out.

    Flip’s click

    Two bars of click, then silence. Keep playing through the gap on any pad: quarters, eighths or a whole groove. Every hit is measured against the grid the click would have played, and at the end you see whether you rushed or dragged.

    Positive means your hits reach the game late (common with Bluetooth audio, TVs and some Android phones). The game subtracts this from every hit.

    You'll hear 4 count-in clicks, then 16 more. Close your eyes and tap any pad on each click. It takes about 12 seconds.

    Keyboard

    D S AHi-hat F RSnare Space G H V B NKick J UTom K L ; IRide / crash Esc PPause

    Each pad has several keys so you can play doubles with two fingers, like sticking on a real kit.

    How to play

    1. Notes fall down five lanes: hi-hat, snare, kick, tom, and ride/crash. Hit the pad as each note crosses the line.
    2. Hollow notes are ghost notes. Play them for bonus points and a quieter sound. Skipping them costs nothing.
    3. Diamonds on the cymbal lane are crashes, small dots are bell hits, everything else is ride. The pad plays whichever sound the chart calls for.
    4. Each hit shows how early or late it was. After the song you get your average offset (rushing or laying back) and your spread, which is how consistent you were.
    5. Fills land on the last bar of every four. A crash on the one follows each fill.
